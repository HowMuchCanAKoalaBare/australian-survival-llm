# Bushfire smoke

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. Not a health department air-quality forecast.
**Confirm with:** your state health smoke advice, saved into Your files, and the air-quality page your state actually publishes. A warning to leave still means leave. Smoke advice is for when you are not in the fire's path.

Smoke can arrive when the fire is a long way off. It is a fine particle problem, worst for babies, older people, anyone pregnant, and anyone with asthma, heart disease or lung disease. The useful moves are indoors, and they are ordinary.

Close windows and doors. Set a split-system air conditioner to recirculate if it has that mode. Turn an evaporative cooler off. It pulls the outside air through the house, which is the opposite of what you want. Don't vacuum, and don't exercise outside because the forecast looks "only a bit grey".

A P2 or N95 respirator, worn so air does not leak around the nose, filters smoke better than a cloth mask or a surgical mask. It is not a reason to stay if the fire warning says to leave, and it does not seal on a baby or on a person who cannot wear it properly. Those people are better in the cleanest room you can make, with the reliever and the action plan if they have asthma. See the asthma note. If someone cannot speak a sentence, is blue at the lips, or has chest pain, call **000**.

This note cannot tell you whether today's air is bad. Use the state environment or health air-quality feed, the emergency broadcaster, or the fire service warning. When the smoke has passed, ash on a roof is not a drinking-water problem boiling will fix. See the water note before you drink the tank.
