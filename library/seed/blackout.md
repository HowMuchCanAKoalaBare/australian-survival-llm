# Power outage

**Confirm with:** your distributor's outage map, and YourHome on batteries and backup: https://www.yourhome.gov.au/energy

## Food

Keep the fridge door shut. A closed fridge holds a safe temperature for a short time, on the order of four hours if it was cold and the door stays closed. A full freezer holds longer than a half-empty one; days, not weeks, and less if it is opened. These are planning numbers, not a guarantee. When power returns, check the food. Do not taste it to see if it is off.

Australia's food-safety temperature danger zone for potentially hazardous food is **5°C to 60°C**. The FSANZ 2-hour / 4-hour guide, in plain words: if that food has been in the danger zone for less than two hours all up, you can refrigerate it or use it. If it has been in the zone for between two and four hours, use it now and do not put it back in the fridge. If it has been in the zone for four hours or more, throw it out. The clock includes every warm spell, not just the latest one. Meat, poultry, seafood, rice, cooked pasta, dairy and leftovers are the foods this is about. Dry goods, uncut fruit and unopened shelf-stable tins are not.

If you are unsure how long the door was open, throw the risky food out. A stomach illness on top of a blackout is worse than a lost chicken.

## Light and heat

Use a torch. Candles start house fires when everyone is tired and the smoke alarm's battery was borrowed for the radio. If you do burn a candle, it sits on a stable surface, away from curtains, and it is out when you leave the room.

Do not heat the house with a gas oven, a charcoal grill, or a camp stove. That is how people die of carbon monoxide, which you cannot smell.

## Generators

A portable generator runs outdoors, in the open, with the exhaust pointing away from the house. Not in the garage with the door up. Not on the verandah. Not in the carport. Not "just to test it". The distance people aim for is several metres from doors and windows, far enough that exhaust cannot be drawn inside. A carbon monoxide alarm with a working battery is part of the setup, not an optional extra.

Symptoms of carbon monoxide poisoning: headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, confusion. Get everyone, including pets, into fresh air and call **000**. Do not go back in to turn the generator off if you are already dizzy. You can become the second body.

Never plug a generator into a wall socket. That backfeeds the street and can kill a lineworker, and it can burn the house wiring. A backup generator connects through a changeover switch fitted by a licensed electrician, so the house is either on the generator or on the grid, not both.

Let the generator cool before you refuel. Petrol spilled on a hot engine is a fire. Store fuel in a proper container, outside, away from the house and from the bushfire plan's woodpile.

## What to run

Decide the load before the outage: fridge, a light, phone charging, a medical device. A generator or a battery is not there to run the oven, the air conditioner and the kettle at once. Write the priority list on paper and put a copy in Your files. See the solar note for batteries and inverters.

## Phones

Low screen brightness, no video, and a voice call uses less than a hotspot. Save the last of the battery for 000. A charged power bank lives in the grab-bag, filled before the bad day, not during it.

## What the solar will not do

If the solar is an ordinary grid-tied system, it has already shut down with the street. A battery only carries the house if backup was installed. See the note on rooftop solar in a blackout. The toilet, if the sewer has backed up, is a separate problem: stop flushing and see the sanitation note.
