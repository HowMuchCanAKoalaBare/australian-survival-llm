# Heatwave

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. Not Bureau of Meteorology advice for a particular day.
**Confirm with:** Bureau of Meteorology heatwave warnings, http://www.bom.gov.au/ and your state health department.

## Who gets into trouble

Australian heat kills people inside houses, often older people, often not "out bush". Babies, pregnant people, anyone with heart, kidney or lung disease, and people who cannot cool their house or fetch water are the ones to check on. A heatwave that lasts several nights is worse than one hot afternoon, because the house never cools.

Never leave a child, an older person or an animal in a parked car. Not for five minutes. Not with the windows cracked. The cabin heats faster than the air outside.

## Staying cooler

Drink water through the day. Don't wait until you are thirsty. If a doctor has told someone to limit fluids, that instruction wins over this note.

Close windows and curtains on the sun side during the day. Open the house up only when the night air is actually cooler than the rooms. A fan helps if the air is not extremely hot and the person can sweat. A fan pointed at someone who has stopped sweating and is confused is not treatment. That person needs cooling and an ambulance.

Cool showers, wet cloths on the neck and groin, and a wet sheet help. Avoid alcohol. Heavy meals and hard work belong in the cool of the morning, or not at all, on a severe day.

Know a cool place you can actually get to: a library, a shopping centre, a neighbour with air conditioning. If the power fails, those public rooms may be the plan. See the blackout note before you run a generator to save the air conditioner.

## Heat exhaustion and heatstroke

Heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, weakness, headache, nausea, cramps, dizzy. Stop, get them into shade or a cool room, sip water if they are fully awake, and cool the skin.

Heatstroke is an emergency. The person is hot and confused, may be vomiting, may have a seizure, and may have stopped sweating. Call **000**. Cool them fast while you wait: shade, strip excess clothing, wet the skin and fan, cool water on the skin, ice packs at the neck, armpits and groin if you have them. If they are not fully awake, do not pour drinks into their mouth.

## Work and sport

Stop. A "she'll be right" job in the sun on a Catastrophic heat day is how people collapse. The Bureau's heatwave warning is the trigger, not how tough the person felt at breakfast.
