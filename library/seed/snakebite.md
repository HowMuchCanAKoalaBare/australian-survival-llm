# Snakebite

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. This is a plain-language reminder, not the ANZCOR guideline.
**Confirm with:** the Australian Resuscitation Council / ANZCOR guidelines you download yourself from https://resus.org.au/ or https://www.anzcor.org/ and keep in Your files.

## What to do

Call **000**. Say snakebite. Australian snakes worth worrying about include brown snakes, tiger snakes, taipans, death adders, black snakes and sea snakes. You do not need the species. Do not catch it, do not kill it, do not take a photo if that means moving the person or getting bitten yourself. Description can wait.

Keep the person still. Lie them down. The venom of the important Australian snakes moves in the lymph when the person walks or waves the limb. Stillness matters more than almost anything else you can do in the paddock.

Apply a broad pressure bandage over the bite, then continue it along the whole limb, from the fingers or toes to the top of the limb, as firm as a bandage for a sprained ankle. You should not be able to slide a finger easily underneath, and you should not stop the pulse. It is not a tourniquet. Do not use rope, wire, or a belt hauled tight. Immobilise the limb with a splint: a stick, a rolled newspaper, the other leg. Leave the bandage and splint on until hospital staff take them off.

Mark the bite site on the bandage with a pen if you can do it without delay. Do not wash the bite. Hospital staff may swab the skin for venom. Do not cut the bite, do not suck it, do not apply ice, and do not give alcohol. Do not walk the person to the car if someone can bring help to them. If you are alone and must move, move as little as possible and keep the limb still.

## What this is not

This is not the treatment for a redback spider. Pressure immobilisation is for snakebite and for funnel-web spider bite, not for redbacks. See the spiders note.

This is not US rattlesnake advice. Do not follow a US article that says to keep the bite below the heart and skip the pressure bandage. If a library article disagrees with this note on Australian snakes, follow this note and the ANZCOR page in Your files.

## On the way

Watch breathing. Be ready to start CPR if they become unresponsive and are not breathing normally. See the first-aid note. Tell the hospital what you saw only if you actually saw it. "It was brown" is a colour, not an identification.
