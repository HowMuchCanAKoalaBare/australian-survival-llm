# Tropical cyclone

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. Not a Bureau of Meteorology forecast.
**Confirm with:** http://www.bom.gov.au/cyclone/ and your local disaster arrangements. Northern Australia: Queensland, the Northern Territory and Western Australia carry almost all the cyclone risk.

## Before it is named near you

The useful work happens at the start of the season, not when the track map turns red. Know the local cyclone shelter plan. Tie down or put away anything the wind can throw: furniture, pots, the kids' trampoline. Clear gutters so rain has somewhere to go. Fill water containers while the tap still works. Charge radios and phones. A battery radio that receives ABC local is more reliable than an app.

If you are in a caravan or a weak building, your plan is to leave for a stronger building when the advice says to, not to ride it out because the van has a bed.

## While it is on you

Stay inside, in the strongest part of the house, away from windows. Do not go out to "have a look" and do not drive. Windborne iron and branches kill people who step outside.

The eye is the calm in the middle. It is not the end. The other side of the cyclone follows, often with the wind from the opposite direction. Stay inside until the official all-clear. People die in the eye because the sky clears and they walk out.

Flooding and storm surge do the damage the wind does not. A cyclone warning and a flood warning can both be true. Do not shelter in a place that also floods. See the flood note. If it is flooded, forget it.

## After

Powerlines down, sewage in the water, creeks up, roofs off. Treat lines as live. Do not use flooded electrical gear. Boil water only if you were told the supply is biologically unsafe and it is not chemically contaminated. Check on neighbours, especially anyone who needed power for a medical device.

Generators stay outdoors, clear of windows and eaves, never in a garage or a closed verandah. Carbon monoxide in a house you have just sealed against a cyclone is a second disaster. See the blackout note.
