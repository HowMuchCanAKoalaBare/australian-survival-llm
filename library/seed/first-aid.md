# First aid basics

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. A reminder, not the ANZCOR guideline and not a first-aid certificate.
**Confirm with:** ANZCOR guidelines from https://resus.org.au/ saved into Your files, and a current first-aid course.

## DRSABCD

Danger: do not become the second patient. Traffic, fire, electricity, a snake still in the grass.
Response: squeeze the shoulders. Talk to them.
Send for help: call **000**. Put the phone on speaker.
Airway: if they are unresponsive, open the airway with a gentle chin lift. Look in the mouth only for something you can see and easily lift out. Do not fish blindly.
Breathing: look, listen and feel for normal breathing for no more than about ten seconds. Gasping is not normal breathing.
CPR: if they are unresponsive and not breathing normally, start compressions in the centre of the chest, about one third of the chest depth, a little faster than one a second (the usual teaching is 100 to 120 a minute). If you are willing and able, give 30 compressions then 2 breaths, and repeat. If you will not give breaths, do continuous compressions. Do not stop except if the scene becomes dangerous, the person starts breathing normally, or you are physically unable to continue. Swap rescuers if you can.
Defibrillation: send someone for an AED if one is close. Turn it on and follow the voice. Keep compressing when it is not analysing or shocking.

## Bleeding

Sit or lie the person down. Direct pressure on the wound with whatever clean cloth you have. If it soaks through, add more cloth on top. Do not lift the first cloth to look. Call **000** if the bleeding is heavy, spurting, or will not slow.

A tourniquet is for life-threatening bleeding from a limb when direct pressure is not enough and you have been taught to use one. It is not the first move for an ordinary cut, and it is not the bandage used for a snakebite. A snakebite bandage must not stop the blood flow. A tourniquet is meant to. Do not mix them up.

## Burns

Cool the burn under cool running water for twenty minutes, as soon as you can. This still helps if you start late. Remove clothing and jewellery that are not stuck. Do not use ice, butter, oil, toothpaste or ointment. After cooling, cover with a clean non-stick dressing or plastic film, not tight, not around a limb like a bandage that stops blood.

Call **000** for burns to the face, airway, hands, feet, genitals or a major joint, for burns that are white, charred or large, for electrical burns, and for chemical burns. Electrical burns can be small on the skin and serious inside. Switch the power off before you touch the person, if you can do that without touching them or the wire.

Chemical burns: brush off dry powder, then flood with water. Do not try to "neutralise" with another chemical. Call **13 11 26** as well as 000 if the person is badly burned by a chemical.

## Choking

If they can cough and talk, let them cough. If they cannot breathe or speak, lean them forward and give up to five back blows between the shoulder blades, then up to five chest thrusts, and alternate. Call 000. If they become unresponsive, start CPR. Do not do abdominal thrusts on a pregnant person; use chest thrusts.

## What to write down

Time of the injury, what you did, any medicines they took, allergies if you know them. Hand that to the paramedics. Do not give someone else's prescription medicine because it "seems right".
