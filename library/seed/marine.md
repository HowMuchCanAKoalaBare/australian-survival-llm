# Stings from the sea

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. Not ANZCOR guideline 9.4.5.
**Confirm with:** the marine envenomation guideline you download from https://www.anzcor.org/ or https://resus.org.au/ and keep in Your files. The points below follow the public June 2025 summary of that guideline. If your copy disagrees, follow your copy.

Get the person out of the water and stay with them. Do not rub the sting. If they collapse, become unresponsive, or stop breathing normally, call **000** and start CPR. See the first-aid note. Do not stand on the beach arguing about the species.

## Do not use the snake bandage

A pressure immobilisation bandage is for a land snake, a sea snake, a funnel-web, a cone shell and a blue-ringed octopus. It is the wrong treatment for a bluebottle, a jellyfish, a stonefish or a stingray. Using it on those stings is a common and serious mix-up.

## Bluebottle

A bluebottle sting is a painful wavy line. It is usually not life-threatening. Do not pour vinegar on it. Vinegar makes a bluebottle sting worse. Pick off leftover tentacles and rinse with seawater, not fresh water. Fresh water can set off stinging cells that have not fired yet.

For the pain, bathe the area in hot water at about 45°C for 20 minutes. That is hot enough to scald a child if you guess. Test it on yourself first. It should be as hot as can be tolerated, not hotter. Do not use a pressure bandage.

Call 000 if the pain is not just in the skin, if they are vomiting, short of breath, or getting worse. If you are not sure it was a bluebottle, treat the next section as possible.

## Box jellyfish and Irukandji

Box jellyfish are a tropical-water emergency: roughly from Bundaberg northwards, across the north, and down the west coast to about Geraldton, with occasional animals further south. A big sting can stop the heart. Irukandji can look like almost nothing on the skin and then, minutes to half an hour later, become severe whole-body pain, vomiting, sweating and difficulty breathing.

If the sting might be one of these, douse the area with household vinegar for 30 seconds, then pick off tentacles. Do not use vinegar on a sting you know is a bluebottle. Do not use a pressure bandage. Do not wash with fresh water. Call **000**. In tropical coastal towns the ambulance and the hospital carry the relevant antivenom. Hot water is not the treatment for this sting.

## Stonefish and stingray

Hot water, same idea as a bluebottle: about 45°C, tested so it does not burn, for about 20 minutes, for the pain. No vinegar. No pressure bandage. Do not go digging for a spine. A stingray barb in the chest or the belly is **000** immediately, and you do not pull it out.

## Cone shell, blue-ringed octopus, sea snake

These are the marine bites that do use the snake method. A blue-ringed octopus bite can be small and almost painless, then paralysis and failure to breathe. A cone shell is the same kind of emergency. Pressure immobilisation, keep them still, **000**. A sea snake is treated as a land snake: still, pressure bandage the whole limb, **000**. See the snakebite note for how firm that bandage is. It is not a tourniquet.
