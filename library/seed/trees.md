# Fallen trees and powerlines

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. Not an energy-network or SES procedure.
**Confirm with:** your distributor's fallen-line advice, and NSW SES or your own state SES if their page says something more specific. Life threat is still **000**.

After an east-coast low, a thunderstorm or a cyclone, a lot of the deaths are the next day, in the cleanup. A hung branch, a tree under tension, and a line on the ground are not small jobs for a tired person with a new chainsaw.

Stay at least 10 metres from a fallen powerline, and further if it is jumping, sparking, or touching a car or a fence. The ground around it can be live. Do not drive over it. Do not spray it with a hose. If someone is in a car touching a line, the usual instruction is to stay in the car unless it is on fire, and then to jump clear without touching the car and the ground at the same time. Call **000** if a person, a fire, or a live line is the problem. Call the electricity distributor, the number on the bill, for a line down that is not immediately killing someone.

A tree on a house, a roof that needs a tarp, and storm damage where nobody is trapped are **132 500**, the SES. They attend by priority. A person trapped under a tree is 000, not a place in the SES queue.

Do not stand under a split trunk or a branch hung up in another tree. Do not cut a tree that is leaning on a line, under tension, or above your head, unless that is work you are trained and equipped to do. A trunk under load springs. Ladders and chainsaws and wet roofs are how helpers become the second patient. If you can keep the rain out of the ceiling from the ground, or by going into the roof space without walking the tiles, do that. Otherwise wait.

A generator used while you wait is outdoors, not in the garage and not on the verandah. See the blackout note. Do not plug it into a wall socket.
