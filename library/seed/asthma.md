# Asthma, including thunderstorm asthma

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. Not a personal asthma action plan and not the National Asthma Council chart.
**Confirm with:** the written action plan for this person, and the current first-aid chart from the National Asthma Council or Asthma Australia, saved into Your files. The plan wins if it names a different reliever.

Sit the person upright. Do not lie them down. Stay with them.

If they have a blue or grey reliever puffer (salbutamol, the Ventolin and Asmol kind of inhaler), use a spacer if you have one. Shake the puffer, fire one puff into the spacer, and have them take four breaths. Do that four times, so four separate puffs. Wait four minutes.

Call **000** and say it is asthma if they are not clearly better, if they are worse, or if they were already severe: unable to speak a sentence, blue at the lips, frightened, or getting little from the puffer. Do not wait out the four minutes before calling when it is already that bad. While you wait for the ambulance, give four puffs every four minutes.

A written action plan overrides this note. Some plans use a combination inhaler as the reliever. Follow that plan. Do not invent a dose of a medicine that is not in the plan and not in your hand.

The published charts say a blue reliever is unlikely to harm in an emergency even when the person has never been diagnosed with asthma. Breathing that is failing is still a 000 call. If there is no spacer, do not delay the call to find one. For an adult, one puff as they take a slow breath is the fallback the charts describe. A spacer is better.

Asthma Australia's information line is **1800 278 462**. It is not a substitute for 000.

## Thunderstorm asthma

In spring, a thunderstorm can rupture grass pollen and drive the fragments deep into the lungs. Victoria's November 2016 epidemic is the one people mean. It also happens in other southern states. People with asthma or hay fever are at higher risk, and so are some people who did not know they had asthma.

On a day the state health department or VicEmergency names as a risk, stay indoors with the windows shut while the storm passes, and have the reliever where you can reach it. Do not go outside for air. This note cannot tell you if today is one of those days. If breathing fails, the first-aid steps above are the same, and 000 is the same.
