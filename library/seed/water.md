## Make a supply before you need one

Store water for drinking and cooking while the tap works. A commonly used household figure is about three litres a person a day for drinking in mild weather, and more in an Australian heatwave. Washing and flushing are extra and can be a lower grade of water. Rotate stored water. Label the date. Keep it out of the sun, in food-grade containers, not in old chemical drums.

A rainwater tank is not automatically safe. Screens stop mosquitoes and animals. A first-flush diverter keeps the dirtiest roof water out. After a bushfire, a dead animal, or a chemical spray drift event, do not drink the tank until you know what fell on the roof. Ash and firefighting chemicals are not removed by boiling.

## Making water safer

The reliable household method for germs is boiling. Bring clear water to a rolling boil for one minute, then let it cool in a clean covered pot. At high altitude boil a little longer. Filter or settle cloudy water through a clean cloth first so you are boiling water, not mud.

Boiling kills germs. It does not remove salt, fuel, pesticides, heavy metals or many algal toxins. If the creek smells of fuel, runs past a mine, a tip, a dip site or a dead animal, or has a blue-green algal scum, do not drink it and do not treat boiling as the fix. Blue-green algae in farm dams and warm still water is a real Australian problem. Boiling can make some algal toxins worse by breaking the cells.

Do not copy a US bleach-drop recipe from memory or from an old FEMA page. Household bleach strengths differ, and a wrong dose is either useless or a chemical burn to the gut. If a state health sheet in Your files gives a dose for a named bleach, follow that sheet. If you do not have it, boil the water.

A filter is only as good as its rating. A cloth stops grit. It does not stop bacteria or viruses. A proper water filter or purifier says what it removes. Read that label before the trip and keep the page in Your files.

## Creeks and remote travel

Australian creeks are shared with cattle, feral pigs, septic seeps and camps upstream. Treat creek water as dirty even when it looks clear. Carry more water than the fine day suggests. In hot country the failure is running out, not the taste.

Seawater and urine are not drinking water. Do not drink them. A solar still is a slow last resort and will not keep a group alive in summer on its own.

## After a flood or a warning

If the water utility or the council says to boil water, do that until they lift it. If they say not to use the water at all, they mean chemicals or sewage the boil will not fix. Follow that, not this note.
