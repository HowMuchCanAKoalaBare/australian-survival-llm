# Calling for help in Australia

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. This is not an official publication.
**Confirm with:** Triple Zero https://www.triplezero.gov.au/ and your state emergency service.

## The numbers that matter

Call **000** for police, fire or ambulance. Say which service you need, then the suburb or place, what has happened, and whether anyone is still in danger. Stay on the line. If you are on a mobile with no signal, try **112**. **106** is the textphone number for people who are deaf or have a speech or hearing impairment.

**132 500** is the State Emergency Service number for storm, flood and similar damage in every state and territory. It is not a substitute for 000 when someone is trapped, injured, or the house is on fire.

**13 11 26** is the Poisons Information Centre, 24 hours, anywhere in Australia. Use it for swallowed medicines, chemicals, plants, bites where you are unsure, and mushroom mistakes. If the person is unconscious, fitting, or not breathing, call 000 first.

**1800 022 222** is Healthdirect, a nurse line when you are not sure the problem is an emergency. It is not for a house fire, chest pain, severe bleeding, or a snakebite.

NSW Rural Fire Service bush fire information line: **1800 679 737**. That number is New South Wales only.

Other information lines, checked against the agency's own pages. These are not rescue numbers. Reporting a fire is still 000. None of them should be guessed into a different state:

- Victoria: VicEmergency Hotline **1800 226 226**. Information, including during major fires. It is not staffed around the clock outside those incidents. The VicEmergency app and ABC Local carry the warnings.
- South Australia: CFS Information Hotline **1800 362 361**.
- Western Australia: DFES information line **13 3337**.
- Tasmania: Tasmania Fire Service Information Line **1800 000 699**.
- Queensland, the ACT and the Northern Territory: this library does not carry a fire-information phone number for them. Use that agency's site and ABC Local. Do not borrow a number from the list above.

**132 500** stays the SES number for storm and flood. If a state page ever publishes a different storm number, the page wins.

## What to say

Give a street address if you have one. On a track, give the road name, the last town, direction of travel, and a landmark. If you have a personal locator beacon, that is more useful than a guessed latitude. Tell them how many people, ages if a child is hurt, and what you have already done.

Do not hang up to "save battery" until the operator says you can, unless the phone is about to die and you still need to pass the location. Then say that and give the location first.

## What this note will not do

It does not list every local brigade, hospital or ranger station. Those change. Put a printed card in the glovebox and a copy in Your files.
