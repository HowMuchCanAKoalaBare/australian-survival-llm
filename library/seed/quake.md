# Earthquake and tsunami

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. Not a Geoscience Australia publication.
**Confirm with:** Geoscience Australia's drop, cover and hold advice, which is CC BY 4.0, Commonwealth of Australia (Geoscience Australia): https://www.ga.gov.au/about/earth-science-week/the-great-shakeout — and the Bureau of Meteorology tsunami warning if one is issued. No endorsement is implied.

Australia does have damaging earthquakes. Newcastle in 1989 is the one people remember. You will not get a useful warning of the shaking itself.

Drop, cover, and hold on. Get down, get under a table or another sturdy piece of furniture, and hold on until the shaking stops. If there is nothing to get under, get next to an interior wall and cover your head and neck with your arms. Do not run for a doorway while things are falling. If you use a wheelchair, lock the wheels if you can and cover your head and neck. Stay in the chair.

When it stops, expect aftershocks. Do not use a lift. If the building is damaged, go outside to an open space away from walls, glass and powerlines once you can do that without crossing a fallen line. See the fallen-trees note. If you smell gas, do not flip a light switch. Get out, and call the gas distributor on the number from your bill, or **000** if you cannot control it.

On the coast, a long or strong shake is itself the warning. So is the sea drawing back, or a roar from the water. Go inland or uphill immediately. Do not go to the beach to look. A tsunami is more than one wave, and the first one is not always the largest. Wait hours, not until the water looks normal. Official warnings come from the Joint Australian Tsunami Warning Centre through the Bureau and the state emergency services, and they are read on ABC Local. This note deliberately does not include a tsunami phone number, because the published digits are easy to garble. Use the warning you can hear, and move if you felt the earthquake.
