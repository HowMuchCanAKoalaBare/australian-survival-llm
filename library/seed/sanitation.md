# Toilets when the pipes fail

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. Not a council waste direction.
**Confirm with:** the instruction your council, the relief centre, or the park gives you for where waste goes. That instruction beats this note.

If sewage is coming up through the drains, or the septic is under floodwater, stop using the toilets. Do not flush. A flood can float a septic tank and leave the lid as a hole in the ground. Stay off septic lids and off ground that has slumped around them.

Hands get washed with soap and water after the toilet and before food. An alcohol rub is only for hands that are not visibly dirty. Gastro on top of a flood puts people in hospital. If there is a boil-water notice, follow it. See the water note.

## A bucket, for a house

Use a sturdy bucket, a strong bag, and a lid. A handful of sawdust, dry garden soil or cat litter on top of the waste cuts the smell. Tie the bag. Keep the bags outside, in a sealed bin, away from food, tanks and children, and where the next rain will not float them into the creek. Do not tip them into the stormwater drain, the gutter or the bush behind the house. Nappies and pads go in bags the same way, not into the garden.

Chemical-toilet waste goes to a dump point, or to the place the council names. It does not go in the street.

## A hole, only if you are remote and nobody is coming

In a town, bag it and wait. Digging a hole in a flooded suburb moves sewage into the drain and the neighbour's yard.

If you are genuinely away from any collection and must dig: at least 30 metres from creeks, tanks, bores and the camp, not uphill of where you take water, and cover it with about 15 centimetres of soil so animals do not open it. A national park's own rule wins over this paragraph. Do not dig in a watercourse because it is soft soil.
