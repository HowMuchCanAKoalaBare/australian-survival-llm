## The temperature rule

Potentially hazardous food wants to be cold, at or below 5°C, or hot, at or above 60°C. Between those temperatures, bacteria multiply. The FSANZ 2-hour / 4-hour guide: under two hours in that zone, you may chill it again; two to four hours, use it immediately; four hours or more, throw it out. Add up every time it was warm, including the trip home and the blackout. See the blackout note for a fridge that has lost power.

Cool hot food in shallow containers before it goes in the fridge, so the fridge stays cold. A big pot of stew in a closed fridge warms everything else.

Throw out tins that are swollen, badly dented on the seam, or leaking. Throw out food that smells wrong, and food that sat in floodwater. Do not taste a suspect stew. Botulism and some other food poisonings are not a "bit off" smell you can judge.

## Preserving without a copied American canning book

This library does not include the USDA Complete Guide to Home Canning. Those times and pressures were written for American kitchens and American jar sizes, and the book is omitted so the model does not recite them as if they were Australian practice.

Drying, salting, fermenting, freezing and high-acid pickling are old methods. They still fail in specific ways.

- Drying needs dry air. Coastal humidity and a lazy sun grow mould. Food should be brittle or leathery the way the method requires, then stored so insects and moisture cannot get back in. Mouldy dried food is thrown out, not scraped.
- Salt and sugar preserve by drawing water out. A guess at "some salt" is not a cure. Follow a tested recipe you have a right to use, stored in Your files.
- Ferments (sauerkraut, some yoghurts) are acidic enough when they actually ferment. A crock that smells rotten rather than sour is not a project. Throw it out.
- Low-acid food in a sealed jar is the dangerous one: vegetables, meat, seafood, and many mixed sauces. Clostridium botulinum grows without air and you cannot see it. Do not invent a processing time. Do not follow a blog. If you bottle meat or vegetables, use a current tested method from a source you have checked, with the right equipment, and keep that document in Your files. A water bath is not a pressure canner. If you do not have the method and the equipment, do not bottle that food. Freeze it, salt it using a known method, or eat it fresh.
- High-acid foods, mostly fruits, and pickles with enough vinegar, are a different and lower botulism risk. They still need clean jars and a method, and they still grow mould if the seal fails.

## Bush food

Do not eat a native plant because a model, a forum, or this note sounded confident. Australian plants include deadly lookalikes. Cycad seeds, oleander, castor oil plant, and a long list of fungi are not a foraging afternoon. See the bush-skills note.

## Cooking when the power is off

A camp stove and a gas barbecue stay outside. Never in the garage, never in the house as a heater. Carbon monoxide and fire. See the blackout note.
