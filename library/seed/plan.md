# A family plan on paper

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. Not the Australian Red Cross RediPlan.
**Confirm with:** RediPlan, which you download yourself from https://www.redcross.org.au/emergencies/prepare/ and keep in Your files. Fill that in. This note is only the skeleton, so a household without the PDF is not starting from nothing.

Write the plan on paper. A note in a phone is gone when the phone is dead. Make two copies. One lives in the grab-bag. One lives with a person outside the region.

## The decisions you make on a calm day

Name an out-of-area contact, and make sure every adult and every older child has that number written down. On a bad day the local towers jam. A text to someone in another city often gets through when a call across town does not. Agree that a single short message is enough: where you are, that you are out, and where you are going.

Name two meeting places. One is just outside the house, for a fire in the kitchen or a night when you get split up on the way to the car. The other is outside the suburb, for the day you cannot get home, and it is a specific corner or a specific relative, not "somewhere in town".

Write who collects each child, and what the school actually does if the roads close. Schools hold children or move them. They do not improvise from a parent's guess. People who need more time, including babies, people with a disability, and households with one car, leave earlier. The trigger for leaving is a fire-danger day you chose in advance, not the moment you can see flame. Leaving early is the plan. Staying to defend is only a plan if you have already practised it and the current warning is not telling you to leave.

## The bag

Medicines for several days, and a paper list of them. Glasses. Phone chargers and a power bank that is already full. A torch. A battery radio, with the local ABC frequency written on it. Water and food that does not need cooking. Cash, because EFTPOS dies. Copies of identification, Medicare and insurance. The plan. A whistle. A small first-aid kit. Nappies if you need them. Pet food and a lead. Spare clothes. A paper map of the roads out.

Pets are part of the plan. Many evacuation centres do not take them. Decide where the animal goes before the day.

Review it at the start of the bushfire season and again before the storm season. Tell the neighbour who lives alone that the plan exists. Put the finished RediPlan, once you have filled it in, into Your files so the local app can search your copy.
