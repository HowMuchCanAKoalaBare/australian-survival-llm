# Bush skills, and why this library will not name dinner

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. Not a flora, and not a bush-tucker guide.
**Confirm with:** a current field guide you own, and state flora or herbarium material. Commercial books stay in Your files.

## Plants

Do not forage Australian plants or fungi from memory, from a chat model, or from this note. The country has plants that look like food and are not. Cycad seeds have poisoned people who thought they had prepared them. Oleander, castor oil plant, and many fungi are not experiments. "I saw it in a book once" is how those stories start.

If you want bush food, buy a legal copy of a proper guide, or a government or research publication that is actually about identification, and put the PDF you are allowed to hold into Your files. Richard Graves' *Australian Bushcraft* and the commercial bush-tucker books are not in this library. The system can search them only after you add your own copy. It still should not override a warning in that book with a cheerful guess.

Useful government-style material, where you can get it under a licence that allows your use, includes state herbarium fact sheets, weed identification pages, and park guides that say "do not eat". Weed pages are good because they tell you what not to touch. They are not a menu.

## Skills that do not involve guessing a berry

Navigation: a map, a compass you have used before the trip, and the habit of looking back so the return looks familiar. A phone GPS is excellent until the battery dies. Carry the paper.

Signalling: a mirror, a whistle, a bright panel, a torch at night. Three signals of anything, repeated, is the old distress pattern. A beacon is better. See the remote-travel note. Do not light a signal fire on a total fire ban or in long grass.

Shelter: get out of the wind and the sun first. In much of Australia the emergency is heat and dehydration, not a snow cave. Shade, water, and staying found beat a stylish humpy. At night in the high country, wool and a groundsheet matter, and wet cotton does not insulate. Change out of wet clothes if you have dry ones. See the cold note if someone is confused and shivering, and the first-aid note. That is 000 country if you can call.

Water: see the drinking-water note. Do not drink from a pretty creek without treating it.

Fire: only where it is legal. A total fire ban means no campfire, including a "small one" and including a trick fire in a tin if the rules say no fire. Escapes start that way.

Knives and tools: cut away from yourself, on a firm surface. A wound a day from help is a bad trade.

## Tracks and private land

A national park, a travelling stock route, a farm, and Aboriginal land are not the same legal thing. Permits and closures are real. A survival note does not give you permission to camp, cut, light a fire, or take water, plants or animals.
