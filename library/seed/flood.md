# Flood and storm

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. Not an official SES publication.
**Confirm with:** your State Emergency Service, 132 500. NSW SES: https://www.ses.nsw.gov.au/

## Water on the road

If it is flooded, forget it. You cannot see the depth, the current, the hole where the road was, or the car already in it. A small hatchback floats in surprisingly shallow, moving water. Turn around. That sentence is the whole driving plan.

Do not walk in floodwater either. Manhole lids come off, drains pull, and the water is sewage, fuel, and whatever was in the shed upstream. Snakes and spiders leave the creek and end up in houses and piles of debris. Treat a bite after a flood the same way as any other bite. See the snakebite note.

Call **000** if someone is trapped, injured, or the water is inside and rising faster than they can leave. Call **132 500** for storm damage, sandbag advice, and leaks when nobody is in immediate danger.

## Before the water arrives

Move what you care about up: medicines, documents, chargers, the animals' food. Know which roads flood on the way to town; local knowledge beats a map drawn in the dry. A grab-bag lives upstairs or in the car, not in the garage that floods first.

If you are told to evacuate, go. Sandbags slow water at a door. They do not hold a river. Fill them only if you still have time to leave afterwards.

## During

Stay out of creeks, storm drains and causeways. Lightning: the safest place is a solid building or a metal-bodied car with the windows up, not under a lone tree, not on a ridge, not in the water. If you are caught in the open, avoid being the tallest object. This is ordinary storm practice, not a promise.

Never run a generator in a garage, on a balcony, or in a tent "just to dry out". Carbon monoxide does not care that you are flooded. See the blackout note.

## After

Do not switch on electrical gear that was under water. That includes the switchboard, the solar inverter and the fridge. A licensed electrician has to see it.

Boil drinking water if the authorities say the supply is dirty, or if floodwater entered the tank, the well, or the house plumbing. Boiling does not remove fuel, pesticides or chemical spills. If the water smells of fuel or chemicals, do not drink it and do not boil it as a fix. Use bottled water or a supply you were told is safe.

Food that sat in floodwater is thrown out, including tins with damaged seams and anything in cardboard. When in doubt, throw it out. Do not taste it to check.

Clean mud out with gloves. The mud is the hazard as much as the water. Wash cuts. If a wound was in floodwater and it becomes red, hot or you feel sick, get medical care. Tetanus is a reason to know whether your immunisation is current, before the flood, not during it.
