# Cold, wet and overnight

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. Not a ski-patrol protocol.
**Confirm with:** the first-aid note, and 000 when you can get through. Most Australian emergencies are heat. This one is the high country, Tasmania, a southerly with rain, and a night you did not pack for.

Shivering, clumsiness, confusion and slurred speech in someone who is wet and cold are hypothermia. When the shivering stops and they go quiet, that is worse, not a recovery. Get them out of the wind and the wet. Dry clothes if you have any. Wool or fleece holds some warmth when damp. Wet cotton does not. Insulate them from the ground with a groundsheet, a pack, or whatever keeps their weight off the dirt. Cover the head.

A warm sweet drink is only for a person who is awake and can swallow. No alcohol. Do not rub their arms and legs. Do not put a confused, profoundly cold person into a hot bath. Call **000** if they are confused, have stopped shivering, are unconscious, or are not improving. Be ready for CPR if they become unresponsive and are not breathing normally.

Stay with the car if you are bogged or snowed, unless the walk out is short, obvious and in daylight. The car is what searchers see. If you run the engine for heat, the exhaust must be clear of snow, mud and a bank. A buried exhaust fills the cabin with carbon monoxide, which you cannot smell. Crack a window. Do not sleep with the engine running. See the blackout note.

Frostbite, a patch of skin that is white, hard and numb, is a winter alpine problem in this country, not a Sydney one. Do not rub it. Warm it against normal skin. If you can call and the skin is frozen hard, call 000. See the remote-travel note before you rely on walking out.
