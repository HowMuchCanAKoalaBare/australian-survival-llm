# Spiders, ticks and stings

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. Not an ANZCOR guideline.
**Confirm with:** ANZCOR first-aid guidelines in Your files, and Poisons Information **13 11 26**.

## Funnel-web spider

A big, dark, glossy spider, often a funnel-web, in eastern Australia, especially a bite with immediate severe pain: treat it like a snakebite. Call **000**. Keep the person still. Pressure immobilisation bandage on the whole limb, splint, do not wash, cut or suck. Funnel-web venom is a medical emergency. Sydney funnel-web country is not the whole continent, but if you are unsure and the spider was a large dark funnel-web type, use the pressure bandage and let the hospital sort the species.

## Redback spider

A redback bite is painful and needs medical care. It is not treated with a pressure immobilisation bandage. Do not bandage the limb the way you would for a snake. Ice or a cold pack on the bite, rest, and get advice. Call **13 11 26**. Call **000** if the person is a small child, is very unwell, is having trouble breathing, or you are not sure it was a redback.

The distinction matters. A pressure bandage is right for a snake and a funnel-web. It is the wrong first-aid for a redback.

## Other bites and ticks

White-tailed spiders and most house spiders cause local pain. Clean the skin, a cold pack, and watch for infection. Do not cut.

A paralysis tick should be removed as soon as you find it. Grasp it close to the skin with fine tweezers or a tick remover and pull steadily. Do not squeeze the body, burn it, or paint it with kerosene. In tick-paralysis country, especially for children, follow the local medical advice you were given. A child who is unsteady, weak, or has double vision after a tick needs urgent care. Call **000** if they are very weak or breathing poorly.

## Jellyfish and bluebottles

Tropical box jellyfish and Irukandji are a different problem from a Sydney bluebottle. Vinegar is used for box jellyfish stings in tropical Australian waters: douse the sting, do not rub, and call 000 if the person is collapsing or it is a tropical jellyfish and you are not sure. Do not use vinegar on a bluebottle as a reflex copied from the tropics; a hot water shower, as hot as the person can tolerate on the skin without burning, is the usual first aid for a bluebottle. If you do not know which animal it was and the person is unwell, call 000 and describe the beach.

## Bees and anaphylaxis

A person who has had anaphylaxis uses their own adrenaline auto-injector if they have one, in the outer mid-thigh, and you call **000**. A second dose may be needed if they were told that and they are not improving. Do not invent a dose. If they have no injector and they are wheezing, swelling in the throat, or collapsing after a sting, that is 000, not a wait-and-see.
