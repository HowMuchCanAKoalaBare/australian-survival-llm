# Mosquitoes after rain and flood

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. Not a state health alert and not vaccine advice.
**Confirm with:** the current state health mosquito warning, saved into Your files. A doctor decides on vaccines. This note does not.

Mosquito numbers jump in the week or two after rain and flood. The illnesses they carry in Australia are prevented by not getting bitten. This note does not name a tablet to take after the fact.

Ross River virus and Barmah Forest virus occur across the country. Fever, joint pain, a rash and a tiredness that can drag on are the usual picture. There is no vaccine for them. Japanese encephalitis has been found in eastern and southern Australia since 2022, tied to waterbirds, pigs and floodwater mosquitoes. A vaccine exists. Whether a particular person should have it is a general practitioner's decision, not a sentence in a field note. Dengue has had local outbreaks in north Queensland. Those mosquitoes also bite during the day, so emptying standing water matters as much as avoiding dusk. Murray Valley encephalitis is uncommon and serious. The prevention is the same.

Use a repellent whose label lists DEET, picaridin, or PMD (oil of lemon eucalyptus), and follow that label for children and babies. Do not invent a concentration, and do not put a homemade oil on a baby because a forum said to. Cover the skin. Sleep under a net if the room is not screened. Dawn and dusk are the worst hours for a lot of bush mosquitoes. Dengue mosquitoes do not stick to those hours.

A rainwater tank with an unscreened inlet, overflow or gap is a mosquito farm above the kitchen. Screen it. A first-flush diverter does not do that job. Pot-plant saucers, buckets and a blocked gutter do the same thing at ground level. Empty them.

After a flood, the bite problem outlasts the water. See the water note before you drink anything the flood touched, and the sanitation note before you decide the drains are fine.
