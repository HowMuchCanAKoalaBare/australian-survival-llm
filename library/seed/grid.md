# When rooftop solar does nothing in a blackout

**Licence:** CC BY 4.0, Australian Survival LLM field notes. Not a wiring guide and not YourHome.
**Confirm with:** the paperwork for the system on this roof, and YourHome on photovoltaic systems and batteries, which is CC BY 4.0: https://www.yourhome.gov.au/energy

A normal grid-connected rooftop solar system produces nothing for the house when the street power is out. The inverter is required to shut down when it loses the grid, so it does not energise a line that a crew thinks is dead. Microinverters do the same thing, one panel at a time. The lights going out is not a fault in the panels. It is what the system was built to do.

A battery does not change that unless backup was part of the installation. Many home batteries only store power for later use while the grid is up, and they shut down in an outage too. Backup means a hybrid inverter, or a gateway or system controller, installed and switched for that job, and usually only the circuits on a backup board. The oven, the ducted air conditioner and the whole house are often not on that board. Read the quote and the shutdown procedure for the equipment you own. If those papers are in Your files, trust them over this note. If they do not say backup, assume the house goes dark with the street.

The panels can still be electrically live in the sun while the street is dead. Do not cut cables, pull connectors apart, or climb a damaged roof to isolate them. Tell anyone working on the house that there is solar. After a fire, ash and a damaged array are a job for the installer, not a ladder and a pair of pliers.

Smoke and a film of ash cut the output on the days you might have wanted it. Clean ash only from somewhere you can stand safely. A solar system does not make a house safe to occupy in a bushfire, and it does not replace leaving early.

What still works in an outage is whatever does not need the dead inverter: a battery lantern, a power bank charged the day before, the car's 12 volt socket while the car is outdoors, and a generator run outside. See the blackout note for carbon monoxide and for never plugging a generator into a wall socket. Any changeover switch is a licensed electrician's job.
